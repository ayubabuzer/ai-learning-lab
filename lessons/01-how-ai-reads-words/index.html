LESSON 01 · LIGHTWEIGHT PLAYGROUND

How Does AI Read Our Words?

When you read a sentence, you see words. A computer needs a way to work with text as numbers. Let's explore the journey using a simple, real browser experiment!

This is a simplified classroom text splitter, not large model or a real LLM tokenizer. The numbers are teaching labels assigned by this page. No model download is needed.

✍️ Your input✍️ مدخلاتك

1 · Write and change your text

Choose an example or write absolutely anything in English or Arabic. Change just one part and compare.

3 · Explore the numbers

Each piece gets a number in this classroom activity. A real language model uses its own tokenizer and token IDs, so its results may be different.

4 · Be a word detective

🧭 Change one word

Change “cat” to “elephant”. Which pieces and IDs change?

🔎 Try another sentence

Choose a different English sentence. Which pieces receive the same classroom IDs?

🤖 Surprise the computer

Add spaces, punctuation or emoji. Can you make the number of pieces change?

What did we learn? Text can be separated into pieces and represented by numbers. Real AI tokenizers use more sophisticated rules; numbers alone do not tell a model what words mean. Next lesson: vectors and relationships.

👩‍🏫 Teacher's 20-minute classroom game

Project the playground. Work in pairs. No programming needed. Use fictional examples, not personal pupil details.

    Ask: Does a piece always equal one whole word? Are these numbers real model token IDs? What would a real AI model need to predict the next word?
