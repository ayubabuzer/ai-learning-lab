LESSON 02 · INSTANT WORD PLAYGROUND

How Does AI Connect Words?

How could a computer notice that cat and dog are related? It can look at the words that appear around them. Let's make and change a tiny word map!

This is a real, working word-context calculation built from the example sentences below. It is a small classroom model, not pretrained embeddings. Results depend on the examples you provide. No internet, accounts or downloads are required.

✍️ Your input✍️ مدخلاتك

1 · Choose two words to investigate

Type any two words that appear in the example sentences. Try a related pair, then a very different pair.

2 · Change what the model sees

These are the sentences used to build the word vectors. Change them, add your own, or repeat a sentence, then rebuild. Does the similarity change?

This experiment measures shared words in sentences. It does not learn meaning the way a large pretrained language model does, and a high score does not prove two words mean the same thing.

4 · Explore the word map

The dots show a two-dimensional view calculated from our context vectors. Words close on this map often share clues. The full vectors have more dimensions; distances on this map are approximate.

📖 Supporting information📖 المعلومات الداعمة

An axes-map example: king, man, woman and queen

Imagine two simple axes: the horizontal direction is about a royal title and the vertical direction groups word forms. We give four words invented coordinates to see how adding and subtracting vectors works.

The arrows show the same change in the coordinates: moving two steps to a royal title, or two steps in the other word-form direction.

king − man + woman = queen

king − man: [3, 1] − [1, 1] = [2, 0]
king − man + woman: [2, 0] + [1, 3] = [3, 3] = queenملكة

These coordinates and axes are invented for teaching only — not actual AI embeddings, not pretrained model outputs, and not a model prediction. Real word relationships are more complex and do not always follow this equation.

Why can one word change the meaning?

The words around “it” help us decide what the sentence probably means.

In a real language model, numerical attention helps tokens combine information from other parts of the sentence. The model learns how to do this from training. A single attention weight cannot prove which noun “it” refers to.

5 · Be a word scientist

🔎 Find a close pair

Compare cat/dog and cat/banana. Which pair shares more context clues?

✍️ Change the evidence

Add a new sentence about two words, rebuild, and see if their score changes.

🧪 Challenge the map

Try a new word. What happens if the examples never mention it?

What did we discover? A vector is a list of numbers. We can compare number patterns to explore relationships. Real embedding models learn much richer vectors. Next lesson: how context helps predict the next token.
