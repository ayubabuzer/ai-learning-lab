LESSON 02 · INSTANT WORD PLAYGROUND

How Does AI Connect Words?

How could a computer notice that cat and dog are related? It can look at the words that appear around them. Let's make and change a tiny word map!

This is a real, working word-context calculation built from the example sentences below. It is a small classroom model, not pretrained Qwen embeddings. Results depend on the examples you provide. No internet, accounts or downloads are required.

1 · Choose two words to investigate

Type any two words that appear in the example sentences. Try a related pair, then a very different pair.

3 · Explore the word map

The dots show a two-dimensional view calculated from our context vectors. Words close on this map often share clues. The full vectors have more dimensions; distances on this map are approximate.

Select a dot to compare it with the second word. The graph updates when you edit the examples.

Unlike a fixed picture, the coordinates are calculated from the words and example sentences you supply.

4 · Change what the model sees

These are the sentences used to build the word vectors. Change them, add your own, or repeat a sentence, then rebuild. Does the similarity change?

This experiment measures shared words in sentences. It does not learn meaning the way a large pretrained language model does, and a high score does not prove two words mean the same thing.

One famous illustration: king, man, woman and queen

Some trained word-vector models capture relationships that can be explored with arithmetic. Here are deliberately invented two-number vectors to illustrate the idea.

king[3, 3]
queen[3, 1]
man[1, 3]
woman[1, 1]

King − Man + Woman ≈ Queen

[3, 3] − [1, 3] + [1, 1] = [3, 1]

These are teaching numbers, not Qwen outputs. The relationship is approximate in some real embedding models, not a rule that always works. Finding the closest vector is also not the same as an LLM predicting its next token.

5 · Be a word scientist

🔎 Find a close pair

Compare cat/dog and cat/banana. Which pair shares more context clues?

✍️ Change the evidence

Add a new sentence about two words, rebuild, and see if their score changes.

🧪 Challenge the map

Try a new word. What happens if the examples never mention it?

What did we discover? A vector is a list of numbers. We can compare number patterns to explore relationships. Real embedding models learn much richer vectors. Next lesson: how context helps predict the next token.

Use pairs or small groups. Ask pupils to make a prediction before running each experiment. No accounts or coding needed.
